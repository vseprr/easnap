# Easnap v1.1.0-beta.1

The first beta of v1.1. It adds **Scrollshot**, a **Settings** window and a
**Turkish** interface, keeps a local log and crash reports, and no longer
loses unsaved work when Easnap exits, Windows signs out or the program
crashes.

This is a prerelease for people who want to try it and report what they find.
The latest stable version is v1.0.2. A beta installs over a stable version and
uses the same settings; to go back, install v1.0.2 again.

## New

- **Scrollshot.** A third mode beside Screenshot and Record. Draw a frame over
  a page, a list or a document, press **Start** and scroll the content
  yourself, with the wheel, the keyboard or the scroll bar. Easnap joins what
  passes through the frame into one tall image.
  - A live preview and the size of the image show it growing. Fixed headers
    and footers are taken once; the mouse cursor never enters the image.
  - When Easnap cannot place a picture without guessing it says so: scroll
    back a little and continue.
  - **Copy**, **Save**, **Edit** or **Discard** when you are done. Enter and
    Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+S saves, Esc discards after asking.
  - **Edit** opens the tall image in the screenshot editor. Every annotation
    tool works on it; the wheel scrolls, Ctrl+wheel zooms, and Copy and Save
    export the whole image at its captured size.
  - Scrolling is manual and vertical. Easnap does not scroll the application
    for you.
- **Settings**, in the menu of the tray icon:
  - language: same as Windows, English or Türkçe;
  - appearance: same as Windows, Light or Dark;
  - the capture shortcut: click the button and press the new combination;
  - start Easnap when you sign in to Windows;
  - a folder for screenshots and one for recordings;
  - save screenshots into that folder without a dialog.
- **Turkish.** The whole interface is available in English and Turkish.
- **About** shows the version and can copy the details a bug report needs.
  **Check for updates** opens the releases page in your browser. Easnap itself
  still connects to nothing.
- **Starting Easnap again** while it runs starts a capture.

## Your work is kept

- **Exit asks** before a running recording, an unsaved recording, a running
  export, an annotated screenshot or a Scrollshot is lost. Staying is the
  default answer.
- **Sign-out and shutdown wait.** Windows shows Easnap's reason, so you can go
  back and save.
- **A finished recording survives.** If Easnap ends before you saved or
  discarded it — a crash, a sign-out, the power button — the next start
  offers it: Save, Delete or Not now. A recording that was still running when
  Easnap stopped cannot be recovered.
- **A recording whose preview does not open can still be saved.** Closing the
  window offers to save it as it is, as an MP4 file.
- **Esc asks** before it discards an annotated screenshot or a Scrollshot.

## Diagnostics, kept on your computer

- A log in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Easnap\logs` (three files of one megabyte). Its
  lines hold no file name, window title or typed text.
- A crash report in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Easnap\crashes` when Easnap or its preview
  helper fails; the three newest are kept. The next start says so once.
  Nothing is sent anywhere. A report holds the call stacks of the program and
  can contain fragments of what was being handled: look at it before you
  attach it to an issue.

## Changed

- Easnap needs **Windows 10 version 2004 (build 19041)** or later and says so
  instead of starting on an older build. The installer has the same limit.
- On the **N and KN editions** of Windows, Easnap now starts without the Media
  Feature Pack. Screenshots and Scrollshot work; Record explains what is
  missing.
- A shortcut that another application holds is reported in a notification, not
  in a dialog at every start. Clicking the tray icon captures in the meantime.
- The "ready" notification appears once per computer instead of at every
  start.
- Messages after a failed recording start say what failed: storage, the audio
  device, the encoder or the capture. A region larger than H.264 allows is
  refused before the recording starts.
- The uninstaller asks whether settings, log and crash reports should be
  removed. It never removes a recording that was not saved.
- An image copied to the clipboard can now be read by .NET applications.

## Fixed

- While typing into a text annotation, a thin vertical line could flash beside
  the caret.
- A failed capture of one monitor produced a black screenshot without a
  message.
- Exit is refused while one of Easnap's dialogs is open. Before, the window
  behind an open Save dialog was destroyed under it.
- The tray icon's image was leaked every time Explorer restarted.
- Temporary folders left without their lock file were never cleaned up.

## Validation

Tested on one development PC: Windows 10 22H2 (build 19045), two 1920x1080
displays at 125 %, NVIDIA hardware decoder and encoder. The results below are
from the development build of this version; the published binaries are built
by the Release workflow from the same sources.

- The Release x64 build succeeds with warnings as errors.
- The Full tier passed on those binaries in a single run: 45 of 45 tests,
  none retried, with the visible-desktop tests on the secondary display. The
  six long-repeat (soak) tests were not run. Earlier runs of the same day are
  in [project status](../project-status.md).
- Scrollshot: the stitching engine is tested against synthetic documents:
  scrolling in both directions, too fast and back, fixed and changing headers,
  a scroll bar and a side rail, repetitive and nearly empty content, pictures
  that load late, noise, random scroll paths and the size limit. On the real
  desktop a second
  process showed a 3,160-row document that was scrolled by wheel, keyboard
  and click; the result was 720 x 2,880 pixels, every pixel equal to what that
  process drew.
- In the running application, with real pointer and keyboard input over a test
  window: the Scrollshot flow with Copy, Save and Edit; the exit, discard and
  sign-out questions; recovery of a recording after the process was ended;
  Settings in both languages including the shortcut; a second start; saving
  without a dialog; a recording with Media Foundation loaded on first use; and
  saving a recording whose preview could not open.
- Thirty unit, integration and private-desktop tests also pass under
  AddressSanitizer. The preview and visible-desktop tests were not run under
  it, and the crash-report test cannot be.

Not tested:

- Windows 11, a Windows N edition, and any build other than 19045.
- Scrollshot on real applications: browsers, editors, lists that load while
  they scroll. It has been verified on a test document only.
- A scale factor other than 125 %, mixed-DPI and portrait layouts, HDR
  displays, another GPU.
- "Start with Windows" in the installed application, the installer itself and
  its uninstall questions, and the package-manager manifests.
- The Turkish text has not been reviewed by a second reader.

## Known issues

- Scrollshot follows content that moves only when you scroll. Video,
  animations or a list that loads more while it scrolls inside the frame can
  make it lose track; narrow the frame.
- Copying and saving a very tall image take a moment during which the island
  does not respond.
- A screenshot can differ from the screen by one level at a few colour values
  while the workspace is visible. Scrollshot is not affected.
- Transient redraw artifacts can occur while resizing or maximizing the Trim
  window.
- GIF preview plays the source recording; its final palette and frame cadence
  are applied during export.

See [troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md) for the questions that come up
most often.
