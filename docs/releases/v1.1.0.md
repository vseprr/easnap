# Easnap v1.1.0

The first stable version of v1.1. Over v1.0.2 it adds **Scrollshot**, which
joins what you scroll through a frame into one tall image, a **Settings**
window, a **Turkish** interface, and a log and crash reports that stay on your
computer. Unsaved work is no longer lost when Easnap exits, Windows signs out
or the program crashes. These notes cover everything that changed since
v1.0.2, including what the prereleases v1.1.0-beta.1 and v1.1.0-beta.2
brought.

## New

- **Scrollshot.** A third mode beside Screenshot and Record. Draw a frame over
  a page, a list or a document, press **Start** and scroll the content
  yourself, with the wheel, the keyboard or the scroll bar. Easnap joins what
  passes through the frame into one tall image.
  - A live preview and the size of the image show it growing. Fixed headers
    and footers are taken once; the mouse cursor never enters the image.
  - When Easnap cannot place a picture without guessing it says so: scroll
    back a little and continue.
  - **Copy**, **Save**, **Edit** or **Discard** when you are done. Enter and
    Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+S saves. Esc, or a right-click outside the frame,
    discards; once you have scrolled for an image, it asks first.
  - **Edit** opens the tall image in the screenshot editor. Every annotation
    tool works on it; the wheel scrolls, Ctrl+wheel zooms, and Copy and Save
    export the whole image at its captured size.
  - Scrolling is manual and vertical. Easnap does not scroll the application
    for you.
- **Settings**, in the menu of the tray icon:
  - language: same as Windows, English or Türkçe;
  - appearance: same as Windows, Light or Dark;
  - the capture shortcut: click the button and press the new combination.
    Ctrl, Alt or the Windows key must be part of it, and a combination that
    another application holds is refused by name;
  - start Easnap when you sign in to Windows;
  - a folder for screenshots and one for recordings;
  - save screenshots into that folder without a dialog. Easnap names the file
    `Easnap_<time>.png`, never overwrites a file and says where it saved.
- **Turkish.** The whole interface is available in English and Turkish.
- **About** shows the version, the shortcut and the Windows build, and can
  copy the details a bug report needs. **Check for updates** opens the latest
  release in your browser. Easnap itself still connects to nothing.
- **Starting Easnap again** while it runs starts a capture.

## Your work is kept

- **Exit asks** before a running recording, an unsaved recording, a running
  export, an annotated screenshot or a Scrollshot is lost. Staying is the
  default answer.
- **Sign-out and shutdown wait.** Windows shows Easnap's reason, so you can go
  back and save.
- **A finished recording survives.** If Easnap ends before you saved or
  discarded it — a crash, a sign-out, the power button — the next start
  offers it: Save, Delete or Not now. "Not now" offers it again at the
  following start. The recovered file is the untrimmed MP4 recording.
- **When the trim window cannot open,** the recording is kept and offered at
  the next start. If Easnap cannot mark it for that, it says that the
  recording is kept only until Easnap starts again and shows where it is, so
  that you can copy it.
- **A recording whose preview does not open can still be saved.** Closing the
  window offers to save it as it is, as an MP4 file. If the place you choose
  cannot be written, Easnap asks you to choose another one.
- **Esc asks** before it discards an annotated screenshot or a Scrollshot.
  Nothing is asked for a screenshot nobody has drawn on.
- A recording that was still running when Easnap stopped cannot be recovered.

## Diagnostics, kept on your computer

- A log in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Easnap\logs` (three files of one megabyte). Its
  lines hold no path, file name, window title or typed text.
- A crash report in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Easnap\crashes` when Easnap or its preview
  helper fails. The three newest are kept, each in a file of its own. The next
  start says so once. Nothing is sent anywhere. A report holds the call stacks
  of the program and can contain fragments of what was being handled: look at
  it before you attach it to an issue.

## Changed

- A shortcut that another application holds is reported in a notification, not
  in a dialog at every start. Clicking the tray icon captures in the meantime.
- The mode options hide while the frame is drawn, moved or resized, and return
  when you let go. When the frame takes their place at the top centre they
  move beside it along the top edge.
- The recording settings panel follows the frame after it was moved or
  resized, unless you have dragged the panel yourself.
- The "ready" notification appears once per computer instead of at every
  start.
- Messages after a failed recording start say what failed: storage, the audio
  device, the encoder or the capture. A region larger than H.264 allows is
  reported while the frame can still be made smaller, instead of failing to
  start with a general message.
- Appearance can follow Windows, also while Easnap runs.
- An image copied to the clipboard can now be read by .NET applications.

## Fixed

- With a frame from the top edge of the screen to the bottom edge, the tools
  appeared on top of the mode options, and moving them away fixed the
  screenshot. The tools now stay outside the frame while anything outside has
  room: below it, above it or beside it; with no room they sit over the bottom
  of the frame. They never cover the mode options, and choosing a tool no
  longer sends them to the other side of the frame.
- With the taskbar at the top of the screen, the mode options were partly
  under it. They now start below the taskbar.
- Easnap's own windows — the islands, the "Copied" and "Saved" notices,
  tooltips, dialogs, the editor's font-size list and its text box — are kept
  out of screenshots and recordings. Windows could nevertheless leave a black
  box of their shape where one had just been shown again or moved, and the
  tooltip of a button on the recording panel could appear in a recording.
  This no longer happens.
- On computers without a hardware H.264 encoder, recordings were stamped one
  frame late and the trim window could refuse to preview them. Easnap now asks
  the software encoder to keep the frames in order.
- Three messages said that temporary media was preserved at a place that
  Easnap deleted when it exited. A recording that cannot be completed now says
  that its unfinished file stays until Easnap exits, and where.
- A failed capture of one monitor produced a black screenshot without a
  message. It is now a failed capture with a notice to try again.
- While typing into a text annotation, a thin vertical line could flash beside
  the caret.
- Exit is refused while one of Easnap's dialogs is open. Before, the window
  behind an open Save dialog was destroyed under it.
- At 150 % display scale, the status message of a Scrollshot and the value
  labels of the editor's options (for example "Boyut 96 pt") were cut off.
  They now take the room their text needs.
- Next to Appearance in the tray menu, Windows drew its own arrow beside
  Easnap's. Only Easnap's is shown now.
- The tray icon's image was leaked every time Explorer restarted.
- Temporary folders left without their lock file were never cleaned up.

## Upgrading from v1.0.2

- **Windows version.** v1.1.0 needs **Windows 10 version 2004 (build 19041)**
  or later. Below that it says so, with the build it found, instead of
  starting, and the installer has the same limit. On an older build, stay on
  v1.0.2.
- **One program.** v1.1.0 installs over v1.0.2 as the same program, with the
  same data folder, and keeps its settings, including the shortcut. It is not
  a second copy beside it. Exit Easnap before you install.
- **settings.ini.** v1.1.0 writes `settings.ini` as UTF-16 text with a
  byte-order mark, so that folder names keep their characters. The folder you
  last saved to in v1.0.2 (`LastSaveDirectory`) is read once and replaced by
  two new entries, one for screenshots and one for recordings.
- **Going back** means installing v1.0.2 again. It does not know what v1.1
  added, such as the language or the two folders, and it no longer finds its
  last save folder, which is now stored under the two new entries. Going back
  has not been tried.
- **New folders.** The log and the crash reports are kept in `logs` and
  `crashes` under `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Easnap`, beside the settings and the
  temporary recordings.
- **The shortcut** is now set in **Settings**; there is no need to edit
  `settings.ini` any more.
- **Language.** The installer's language applies to its own pages. Easnap
  follows the language of Windows until you choose one in Settings.
- **Uninstalling.** The uninstaller now asks whether settings, log and crash
  reports should be removed; keeping them is the default. It never removes a
  recording that was not saved.
- **N and KN editions** of Windows: Easnap no longer needs the Media Feature
  Pack to start, as v1.0.2 did. Screenshots and Scrollshot work without it;
  Record says that it needs it. This follows from how the program is built and
  has not been tried on such an edition.

Both packages contain the same two executables. Easnap needs Windows 10
version 2004 or later, x64. The binaries and the installer are **unsigned**,
so Windows may display a publisher or reputation warning; on Windows 11 with
Smart App Control turned on, Windows can block them.

To check a download:

```powershell
Get-FileHash .\Easnap-v1.1.0-windows-x64-setup.exe -Algorithm SHA256
gh attestation verify .\Easnap-v1.1.0-windows-x64-setup.exe --repo vseprr/easnap
```

Exit Easnap before installing over, or replacing, an existing copy.

## Validation

- **In the test lab,** virtual machines with Windows 10 22H2 and no GPU, on
  the sources of this version: all 54 tests, including the long repeats, at
  125 % display scale, and the 48 of the Full set in a second image at 150 %.
  In the first run at 125 % one timing check of the recording engine failed:
  its capture clock woke late once while tests ran in parallel. It had passed
  in the 15 lab runs before, and the repeated run passed all 54. One recording
  test used its fallback capture, as it does on a still desktop.
- **By the owner,** on the development PC, with a release candidate that was
  not published: installing over beta.2, Start with Windows, removal,
  Scrollshot, the black boxes and the Turkish text. The check found two
  problems, both corrected in this version: the second arrow in the tray menu,
  and Windows painting its own title bar over Settings and the message
  dialogs after they were moved, which the candidate had brought in. The test
  that drags those dialogs failed on the development PC before the correction
  and passed after it.
- **An upgrade from v1.0.2,** in the lab with the candidate's installer, both
  installers silent: Windows then listed one Easnap and the settings file was
  unchanged.
- Not checked: Windows 11, an N edition, going back to v1.0.2. The
  [validation records](validation-history.md#validation-of-v110) list every
  run, and [project status](../project-status.md#not-validated) what has not
  been checked.

## Known issues

- On GitHub's test machines, which have no GPU, a trim export of a recording
  with audio failed now and then; the cause is not known yet. If an export
  fails, try it again.
- Scrollshot follows content that moves only when you scroll. Video,
  animations or a list that loads more while it scrolls inside the frame can
  make it lose track; narrow the frame.
- Copying and saving a very tall image take a moment during which the island
  does not respond.
- A screenshot can differ from the screen by one level at a few colour values
  while the workspace is visible. Scrollshot is not affected.
- Transient redraw artifacts can occur while resizing or maximizing the Trim
  window.
- GIF preview plays the source recording; its final palette and frame cadence
  are applied during export.

See [troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md) for the questions that come up
most often.
